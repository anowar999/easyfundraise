Most founders start fundraising by collecting names. They end up with a spreadsheet of 400 investors, half of whom have never written a cheque at their stage. The result is weeks of emails and very few replies.

The founders who raise fastest do the opposite. They build a short list of investors who are a near-perfect fit, and they reach each one personally. Here is how to do that.

## 1. Decide what you are raising before you look for who

Write down four things before you search:

- **Stage:** angel, pre-seed, seed or Series A.
- **Round size and cheque size:** a $1.5M seed round usually means one lead writing $500K to $1M and five to fifteen smaller cheques.
- **Sector:** the one or two sectors an investor would file you under.
- **Geography:** where your company is incorporated and where you will hire.

These four answers remove more than 90% of investors from your list, which is exactly the point.

## 2. Filter for investors who invest at your stage

An investor's website says "seed to growth". Their portfolio says they have not done a seed deal in three years. Always check what they actually did recently.

Good signals that an investor is active at your stage:

- A new investment announced in the last 12 months at your stage.
- A partner who writes or speaks about your sector.
- A fund raised in the last two years (they have money to deploy).

## 3. Separate leads from followers

At seed, most investors will not commit until someone else has set the terms. Mark which investors on your list **lead rounds** and start with them. Once you have a lead, followers become much easier.

## 4. Find the right person, not just the firm

Emailing a firm's general inbox rarely works. Find the partner or principal who covers your sector. If an associate covers it, that is fine: associates source deals and can champion you internally.

## 5. Look for warm paths, then go cold with confidence

A warm introduction from a founder the investor backed is the strongest route. But a short, specific cold email works far better than most founders expect, especially at pre-seed and seed. Read our guide to [cold emailing investors](/blog/cold-email-to-investors) for templates.

## 6. Keep the list small and the follow-up tight

Fifty well-matched investors, each contacted personally and followed up twice, will beat 400 generic emails. Track every conversation in a [fundraising CRM](/#crm) so nobody goes cold between follow-ups.

## Where to find investors

- **Investor databases** such as EasyFundraise, with filters for stage, sector, cheque size and geography.
- **Portfolio pages** of companies one or two years ahead of you: their seed investors are likely to back you too.
- **Angel networks and syndicates** in your city.
- **Accelerator demo day lists** and investor newsletters.
- **Founders you know**: ask who was most helpful in their round.
