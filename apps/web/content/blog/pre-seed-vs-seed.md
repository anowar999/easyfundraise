Pre-seed and seed sound similar, but investors judge them very differently. Pitching a seed fund with a pre-seed story, or the other way round, is one of the most common reasons founders hear "too early".

## Pre-seed

- **Typical round:** $250K to $1.5M, often on a SAFE.
- **Who invests:** angels, angel syndicates, pre-seed funds and accelerators.
- **What they back:** the team and the insight. Early signals help: a waitlist, design partners, letters of intent, a working prototype.
- **What to show:** why this problem, why you, why now, and what you will prove with the money.

## Seed

- **Typical round:** $1.5M to $4M, priced or on a SAFE.
- **Who invests:** seed VCs, often with one lead and several followers, plus angels.
- **What they back:** early traction and a credible path to Series A. Revenue, usage growth, retention and a clear go-to-market.
- **What to show:** the numbers, the repeatable motion, and the 18 to 24 months of milestones the round funds.

## Angel rounds

An angel round is a set of individual investors, usually writing $10K to $100K each. It can be your whole pre-seed or sit alongside a fund. Angels decide quickly and personally, so a short, warm email works well.

## How to tell which one you are raising

Ask yourself: could you show a seed investor the metrics they need for a Series A plan? If not yet, raise pre-seed and target investors who say so publicly.
